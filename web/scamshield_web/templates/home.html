A second look at every email your parents get.

ScamShield checks the links in new Gmail messages against real evidence: how old the website is, and whether Google lists it as dangerous. When an email is a scam, it gets labeled in Gmail and they get a plain warning, with you copied.

How it works

  1. A new email arrives

    ScamShield looks at new messages in their Gmail inbox on a regular schedule. Emails without links are left alone.

  2. Every link gets checked

    It looks up how long each website has existed and asks Google Safe Browsing whether the link is known to be dangerous.

  3. Scams get labeled and explained

    Risky emails get a “ScamShield” label in Gmail, and they get a short warning that says why, in plain words. You get a copy.

What it never does

  • Delete, move or send their email.
  • Show you their emails. You see warnings only.
  • Ask for their password. They sign in on Google’s own page.

Set it up together

You fill in a short form and send them a link. They open it and connect their own Gmail. It takes about five minutes.
